Photographing prints and album pages

Chapter 2 of 10 · 4 scenes · about 40 seconds

0:00

    The steps in this chapter

    1. Use natural lightDaylight works best. Spotlights and lamps cause glare on glossy prints: a bright patch on the screen. Move the print, or yourself, until the patch has gone. Keep the light even, and leave the flash off: strong sunlight across part of the backing can confuse the edge finder.
    2. Hold it level, watch for shadowsHold the phone flat, straight above the print, so it looks square on the screen. StoryPrints can straighten a photo taken at an angle, but holding the phone flat gives the best result. Watch for your own shadow or the phone’s. Stand so the light isn’t behind you.
    3. A contrasting backing, and a borderA backing that contrasts with the photo helps StoryPrints find the edges more easily: dark for light pictures, such as sky or beach, light for darker ones, such as indoor shots. Always leave some backing showing all round the print. It helps, but it isn’t essential.
    4. Album pages: leave the photos inThere’s no need to take photos out of the album. If the page has a clear plastic cover, peel it back if you can: it causes reflections. Get as much of the page in as you can. It’s fine if a little of the table shows.

    Any phone camera will do; use its normal photo mode at full quality. A few minutes setting up saves a lot of adjusting later.
